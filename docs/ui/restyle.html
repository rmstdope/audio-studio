A · Aurora. Today's dark studio, made softer and richer.
  • Translucent "glass" panels on a deep background with two faint colour glows; rounder corners (16–20 px).
  • The orange accent becomes an orange-to-pink gradient, used only for the main action and the logo.
  • The act's progress is a ring with the big count beside it; the status counts are pills.
  • Cast cards lift on hover; the delivery cue sits in a code-like well.
  • Closest to today in layout: the sidebar, header and sections stay where they are, so it's mostly a stylesheet change.
B · Graphite. Quiet and dense, like a pro tool (think Linear or Raycast).
  • A 52 px icon rail for the workspaces, with a narrow act list beside it showing each act's count and a thin progress line. Together they take 248 px, about as much as today's sidebar, but they hold more.
  • A top bar with the breadcrumb, a search (⌘K) and spending; smaller type and hairline borders throughout.
  • Languages are underlined tabs; the counts are one strip of cells over a segmented bar.
  • The cast is a table instead of cards: more characters fit on screen, which suits RetroQuest's larger cast.
  • The biggest change to layout and navigation, and the most room for content.
C · Arcade. The studio dressed like the game it makes.
  • A pixel font (Silkscreen) for titles, buttons and numbers, an italic serif for delivery cues, and Geist for the reading text.
  • The game's own 16-colour palette (lamp yellow, amber, leaf green, wine) on deep purple, with faint scanlines.
  • Hard 2 px borders with offset black shadows; buttons press in when clicked.
  • The progress is a brick "health bar"; each character gets a little pixel portrait and an HP bar.
  • Most character; less neutral for long sessions, and the pixel font is for short labels only.
